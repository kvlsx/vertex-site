What It Does

One window instead of eight

Vertex owns the frame — the rail, the title bar, notifications and settings. Inside, each service renders itself exactly as it does in a browser.

01

Isolated sessions

Every service gets its own storage partition. Two work accounts and a private one in the same messenger, all signed in at once, none of them aware of the others.

02

System notifications

Banners come from macOS itself, so dismissing one does not drag you into the app. If you switch notifications for Vertex off in System Settings, nothing appears — that setting has the last word.

03

130 services ready

Telegram, Slack, WhatsApp, Discord, Gmail, Notion, Figma, Linear, ChatGPT, Zoom, Nextcloud and more from the catalog — or paste any URL and it becomes a service.

04

Sleeps when idle

Inactive services are throttled and put to sleep after a few minutes, so a dozen open services do not cost you a dozen services' worth of CPU.

05

Extensions

Content blocking, cookie banners, proxy. The registry is data only — plain JSON, no executable code is fetched or run.

06

Yours to colour

Eleven palettes, each in a dark and a light version, plus twelve colourways for the app icon. Pick a look and the whole frame follows.

07

Your own CSS and JavaScript

Every service can carry its own stylesheet and script, kept as plain files you edit in your editor. Restyle Element, hide what you never use, add the shortcut you miss.

08

Drag to reorder

Hold an icon in the rail and move it; the others slide aside. Vertex remembers the order, and a plain click still just opens the service.

Install

Getting it running

Download the DMG, drag Vertex into Applications. One extra step the first time — please read it, otherwise the app looks broken when it is not.

macOS will block the first launch

Vertex is signed ad-hoc, not with an Apple Developer ID — that certificate costs $99 a year and this is a free app. macOS therefore greets the first launch with “Vertex is damaged and can’t be opened”. The download is fine; macOS simply refuses to vouch for it.

Right-click Vertex.app and choose Open, then confirm. Or clear the quarantine flag once:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Vertex.app

After that it launches normally. Only the very first run is affected.

FAQ

Questions

Is Vertex really free?

Yes. There are no paid plans, no trial and no feature held back.

Why does macOS say the app is damaged?

Vertex is signed ad-hoc instead of with a paid Apple Developer ID. The download is fine — open it once with right-click → Open, or run the xattr command above.

Where are my logins stored?

On your Mac, in a separate storage partition for every service. Removing a service deletes its data too.

Can I add a site that is not in the catalog?

Yes — paste any web address and it becomes a service with its own session.

Does it run on Intel Macs?

Not at the moment. Builds are for Apple Silicon (arm64), macOS 12 Monterey or newer.

What does the QR sync transfer?

The list of services (name, address, mute and notification switches), language and a few settings. No logins, cookies or custom code.
